Corey Wayne, Casey Zander and Hinge's own data all say the same core thing: ask early, name a specific plan, keep it light, and don't hang on the outcome. This page is the playbook, plus reps to actually do it.

01

The short version

Six rules that every source agrees on. The rest of this page is detail.

  1. Ask early. On Hinge, within 5–10 good messages or 1–3 days. In person, in the same conversation.
  2. Be specific. An activity plus a day. “Coffee Saturday?” beats “we should hang out sometime.”
  3. Light before the ask, sincere during it. Playful teasing gets you there. The ask itself is plain and clear.
  4. Small first date. Drinks, coffee or a walk. About an hour, easy to leave or extend.
  5. Text is for logistics. Don't build the relationship over text. Save it for the date.
  6. Stay outcome-independent. A no, a flake or silence says something about fit or timing, not about you. Reply graciously once, then move on.
02

What “chill” actually means

Being chill isn't acting like you don't care. It's being relaxed, playful and clear about wanting to see her, and being fine with either answer.

RelaxedYou're having a good time whether or not this becomes a date. No rushing, no over-explaining.
PlayfulLight teasing and a little banter. You don't take yourself too seriously.
ClearWhen you ask, you actually ask. “We should hang out sometime” isn't chill. It's just unclear.

The “bratty little sister” idea, in context

Corey Wayne does use this line. He means teasing her the way kids tease someone they like: calling her a dork, or joking that she's “blowing her chances” when she trips. It is not being mean or dismissive. His ratio is roughly 90% charming and confident, 10% teasing. If you tease more than you're warm, it reads as a put-down.

Is the teasing landing?

She…MeansDo
Teases back, says “haha,” runs with the jokeIt's workingKeep going
Gets short, explains herself earnestly, goes quietIt stung or didn't landDial it back, be sincere

Tease what she chose, like her prompt answer, a hot take or her taco opinions. Never tease what she can't change: her body, her job, her family.

Take this / leave that Take the mechanics: be direct and playful, lead the plan, don't chase. Leave the game-playing, like replying slowly on purpose or treating dates as a scoreboard. Being clear and warm gets the same result without the act.

Where I'd push back on the coaches
  • Deliberately slow replies: just reply when it's convenient. Not staring at your phone does the same job.
  • Wayne's “that's the only night I'm free” test: if she offers a real alternative day, that's interest. Take it.
  • The fake “running late, can we push it back?” confirm text: skip it. “Still good for 7?” does the same job honestly.
04

Hinge playbook

Go from match to date in 5–10 messages. The whole chat should fit on about one phone screen before you ask.

1CommentLike + comment on a prompt
2Banter2–4 exchanges
3AskActivity + day
4Lock it inTime, place, number
5ConfirmOne line on the day

1 · The opener: always a comment, never a bare like

Comment on a prompt, not a photo. Formula: react to what she said + a playful angle or a small challenge.

Her promptWeakBetter
“Weirdly attracted to… people who can cook”I can cook!Define ‘cook.’ Does a very confident grilled cheese count?
“Most irrational fear… geese”Haha sameCorrect fear. Geese are the only animals that have never lost a fight.
“Together we could… find the best tacos in SLC”I love tacosBold of you to assume you'd win that argument. What's your current #1?
“Unusual skills… parallel parking”NiceBig claim. I'm going to need proof at some point.
Travel photo promptWhere is this?This looks like the opening of a heist movie. Were you the mastermind or the getaway driver?

2 · The banter: 2–4 exchanges

  • Keep messages short: 1–2 sentences, roughly matching her length.
  • Mix statements and questions. All questions feels like an interview. All statements feels like a monologue.
  • Build one running joke if one appears. It's the best bridge into the ask.
  • Green lights: she asks you questions, writes as much as you or more, laughs or riffs, and replies within a day.

3 · The ask

Formula: a callback to the chat + an activity + one or two days. No “would you maybe possibly want to…”

Ok, the geese debate needs to be settled in person. Drinks Thursday or Saturday?
You're fun to talk to. Let's continue this over coffee. Free Sunday afternoon?
I'm holding you to that taco ranking. [Place] Thursday at 7?
I'd like to meet you. Drinks this week? I'm free Wednesday or Thursday.

4 · Lock it in  ·  5 · Confirm

Great. Thursday, 7pm at [place]. What's your number? I'll text you so you have mine.
Hey, it's Trent from Hinge. See you Thursday.
Still good for 7 at [place]?

Only send the day-of confirm if the date was set more than a couple of days ahead. Calling instead of texting to set it up (Wayne's way) is great if you like phones, but it's optional.

In-person playbook

Open with something about the situation, talk for 3–10 minutes, then ask for her number with a reason attached.

1 · Open with the situation, not her looks

WhereOpener
Coffee shop line“Is the [drink] here actually good, or am I about to make a mistake?”
Bookstore / library“Ok, I have to know. Is that one worth it? I've picked it up three times.”
Grocery store“You look like you know produce. How do you tell when an avocado's ready?”
Party / friend's event“How do you know [host]?” This is the easiest opener there is.
Class, group, event“Is it always this [crowded / quiet / long]?”
Trail / dog park“Your dog is winning the park today. What's their name?”
Anywhere“Hey, I noticed you and wanted to say hi. I'm Trent.” Said calmly and with a smile, this beats any clever line.

2 · The short conversation

  • 3–10 minutes. Long enough to feel a spark, short enough to end on a high note.
  • One light tease is plenty: “You're very confident about avocados. Suspiciously confident.”
  • Say your name early and use hers once.
She's into itShe turns toward you, asks questions back, laughs, adds to the conversation, and stays when she could leave.
Wrap it upOne-word answers, eyes on her phone or the door, turned away, headphones back in. “Nice talking to you, enjoy your day.” That is the chill move.

3 · The ask: a reason + her number

I've got to run, but I've enjoyed talking to you. Can I get your number? I'd like to take you for coffee this week.
You're fun. Want to grab a drink this week? What's your number?
Want to get lunch after [next class / meeting]?

Hand her your phone with a new contact open, then text her right away so she has your number: “Hey, it's Trent from the coffee line.”

4 · Follow up within 24 hours, with a plan

Hey, it's Trent from [place]. Still thinking about your avocado technique. Coffee Saturday morning?
Ground rules
  • Don't open on someone who's stuck with nowhere to go (a train, a checkout line) unless you keep it very brief.
  • Headphones in means do not disturb. Respect it.
  • Work is different. Only ask someone you already have rapport with, and never someone you work with directly or who reports to you. Ask once, and handle any answer without awkwardness afterward.

First-date defaults in SLC

60–90 minutes, public, easy to talk, easy to extend. Keep two or three spots ready so the ask takes five seconds.

Utah tip Plenty of people here drink neither alcohol nor coffee. If you can't tell, offer something neutral, like “drinks or a soda run, your pick,” or a walk.

  • You pick the place. Leading the plan is part of the ask.
  • Plan to pay for a small first date, but don't make a big deal of it.
  • Pick somewhere close to a next spot, in case it's going well.
  • Check the hours before you suggest a place.
08

Before you send the ask

Most dates that never happen die the same way: the chat ran too long, the ask was too vague, or you came on too eager. Run this before you hit send.

Mistakes to avoid

  • The pen-pal trap: a week of great chat and no plan.
  • The vague ask: “we should hang out sometime” hands all the planning to her.
  • The over-apologetic ask: confidence is just leaving out the disclaimers.
  • Double-texting after silence: one nudge is fine, but a second one is chasing.
  • Teasing that stings: if you have to explain it was a joke, it wasn't a good one.
  • Arguing with a no: thank her and let it go.
